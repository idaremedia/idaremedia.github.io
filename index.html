Featured work

Three projects, and what each one taught me

Hearts of Iron IV

A four-episode comedy series on a 20-hour week

The idea. Strategy players spend hundreds of hours on serious world-war campaigns, then share the most absurd things that happened in them. The side stories in the Yakuza games prove a serious world becomes more loved when you let it be ridiculous for ten minutes. So instead of explaining the game, the series told the stories players already tell each other: "I Got Reincarnated as a Field Marshal and Now I'm Stuck on a Beach".

What I did. Concept, script, visual approach, edit and publishing for all four episodes, alongside the regular trailer schedule.

What I learned. Episodes that open on a moment players have lived themselves land faster than ones that need context, so every brief since starts with a recognisable first three seconds.

Hearts of Iron IV

Anniversary Sale: finding the hook in a ten-year-old trailer

The problem. The sale short had the DLC animation, official trailers and gameplay, but the opening felt weak.

What I did. I went back through every official Hearts of Iron IV trailer to the 2016 announcement. The very first one ran real footage under a Churchill speech, so I kept only the speech as the tension build for the new short.

What I learned. Your own archive is full of hooks, and every hook makes a promise: some viewers read it as a Hearts of Iron V announcement, a reminder to know exactly what the first seconds tell people.

Victoria 3

Launching a TikTok channel from zero

The start. The first video was an engagement piece to say hello to players, then the channel found its rhythm in sale videos with an entertaining twist and announcements of every kind, including the open beta.

What worked best. Trains. A video about the railways of Victoria 3 for Steam's Train Fest brought more engagement than anything else on the channel: a game about economies and politics becomes exciting in thirty seconds the moment you find the thing its players already love talking about.

What I would change. Give Victoria 3 its own engagement content and the same focus as Hearts of Iron IV, instead of mostly content that travels with a sale.
